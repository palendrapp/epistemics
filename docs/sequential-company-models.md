# Mathematical models for sequential company evaluation

Status: model-family specification. A constrained first subset is now implemented in the [company dossier pilot](company-pilot.md): joint inference, positive/negative evidence weights, redundant-evidence weighting and an auxiliary-prior alternative. The remaining models below are proposals. These are original behavioral specifications motivated by the cited literature, not validated instruments or assertions about a language model's internal representations. The original battery and v1 report remain available; the new pilot uses a separate v2 report.

The design proposal is in [sequential-company-evaluation-proposal.md](sequential-company-evaluation-proposal.md). The equations below distinguish source impressions, evidence integration, reported probabilities, uncertainty reports and decisions. Separating these mathematically does not guarantee that their parameters are identifiable from observations.

## 1. Notation and scope

Suppress episode and agent indices initially. Let:

- \(H\in\{0,1\}\) denote a fixed, resolvable favorable outcome, such as next-year revenue growth exceeding a threshold.
- \(D_t=(E_1,\ldots,E_t)\) be the evidence actually available by step \(t\), including relevant provenance and dates.
- \(s_t\) denote the source, \(x_t\) a centered vector of source/presentation features, and \(T_t\) randomized skeptical/negative source framing.
- \(p_0\) be the initial probability under the specified task model.
- \(z_t\) be a latent descriptive assessment on a log-odds scale, and \(m_t=\sigma(z_t)\).
- \(\hat p_t\) be the observed probability report and \(a_t\) the observed action.
- \(\sigma(z)=1/(1+e^{-z})\), \(\operatorname{logit}(p)=\log[p/(1-p)]\).

Distinguish three manipulations: negative source framing \(T_t\), unfavorable factual evidence \(N_t\), and negative wording of the response question \(W_t\). Randomize framing independently of factual direction. Translate complementary questions back to the same event \(H\) before analysis. If these manipulations always occur together, separate negativity parameters are not identifiable.

The reference likelihoods below are justified only in a controlled condition where the respondent has access to the relevant model/calibration information. In an open-ended discovery condition, likelihoods are model assumptions to compare, not an evaluator's privileged answer key.

## 2. Model A: source impressions and weighted evidence accumulation

### 2.1 An operational source-impression model

Define \(r_t\) as perceived probability that this evidence item is generated by an informative process, rather than a specified noise process:

$$
r_t=\sigma\left[
\operatorname{logit}(r^{\mathrm{base}}_{s_t,t})
+u_{s_t}
+\beta_N^{\mathrm{src}}T_t
+\boldsymbol\beta_X^\top x_t
\right].
$$

The baseline \(r^{\mathrm{base}}\) reflects public calibration/track-record information. \(u_s\) is a residual source effect, constrained to have mean zero; \(\beta_N^{\mathrm{src}}>0\) means greater perceived validity for negatively or skeptically framed sources with factual content held fixed. Format, provenance and credential terms can enter \(x_t\), but only a few should be free in the first study.

This validity probability is not the source's probability of making a correct binary claim. If informative reports are 90% correct and uninformative reports are 50% correct, overall correctness is \(0.9r_t+0.5(1-r_t)\). Calibration material and trust questions must use the same operational definition.

The framing variable here is an exogenous source/presentation attribute. Substituting the observed claim's sign into a pre-evidence reliability prior without a proper joint model can count the same information twice. Content-dependent trust revision belongs in Model B, or must be explicitly treated as a descriptive post-evidence gate.

For a new independent item, let \(f_h(E)\) be its informative likelihood under \(H=h\), and \(g(E)\) its uninformative likelihood, independent of \(H\). Its subjective log likelihood ratio is:

$$
\ell_t(r_t)=
\log\frac{r_t f_1(E_t)+(1-r_t)g(E_t)}
{r_t f_0(E_t)+(1-r_t)g(E_t)}.
$$

For a symmetric binary report of a negative outcome, with 90% accuracy when informative:

$$
\ell_t(r_t)=\log\frac{0.1r_t+0.5(1-r_t)}
{0.9r_t+0.5(1-r_t)}.
$$

Model A uses an item-level validity gate conditional on public history. Persistent uncertain source types require joint inference across items, as in Model B.

### 2.2 Dependence and duplicate counting

For the general dossier, calculate two likelihood ratios under the specified source model:

$$
\ell_t^{\mathrm{inc}}=
\log\frac{P(E_t\mid H=1,D_{t-1})}
{P(E_t\mid H=0,D_{t-1})},
\qquad
\ell_t^{\mathrm{iso}}=
\log\frac{P(E_t\mid H=1)}
{P(E_t\mid H=0)}.
$$

These condition on the known source/presentation features; notation suppresses them. Both must be computed from the same explicit generative model. Incremental evidence accounts for the provenance graph; isolated evidence treats an item as if encountered alone.

Define:

$$
\tilde\ell_t=(1-\delta)\ell_t^{\mathrm{inc}}+\delta\ell_t^{\mathrm{iso}},
\qquad 0\leq\delta\leq1.
$$

For an exact, known, unselected copy of a previously seen observation, \(\ell_t^{\mathrm{inc}}=0\). With \(\delta=0\) the copy adds no evidence; with \(\delta=1\) it is counted again at standalone strength. Independent evidence has equal incremental and isolated likelihood ratios, so \(\delta\) has no effect.

Source selection or the decision to republish can itself be informative. The zero-increment claim therefore requires a controlled copy mechanism, not merely similar wording. A deterministic model calculation on known data similarly adds no new observation to an ideal unlimited reasoner, although it can help a bounded agent extract implications. Include extraction/comprehension checks before attributing such effects solely to double counting.

### 2.3 Updating the company assessment

Set \(z_0=\operatorname{logit}(p_0)\) and \(z_t^-=z_0+\rho(z_{t-1}-z_0)\). Let \(\ell_t^{\mathrm{iso,ref}}\) be the standalone likelihood ratio under the prespecified reference model. Define \(N_t=I(\ell_t^{\mathrm{iso,ref}}<0)\) for unfavorable factual evidence and \(C_t=I(z_t^-\ell_t^{\mathrm{iso,ref}}>0)\) for congruent evidence; ties are zero. Thus even an uninformative copy has a consistently coded factual direction. Then:

$$
g_t=\exp\left(\beta_N^{\mathrm{ev}}N_t+\beta_C C_t\right),
$$

$$
\boxed{
z_t=z_0+\rho(z_{t-1}-z_0)+\kappa g_t\tilde\ell_t
}
$$

Interpretations:

| Parameter | Interpretation within this model |
| --- | --- |
| \(\kappa>0\) | Overall sensitivity to evidence |
| \(\beta_N^{\mathrm{ev}}\) | Additional weight on adverse factual evidence; negative/positive multiplier is \(e^{\beta_N^{\mathrm{ev}}}\) for otherwise matched conditions |
| \(\beta_C\) | Additional weight on congruent evidence |
| \(0\leq\rho\leq1\) | Retention of accumulated evidence relative to the starting assessment |
| \(0\leq\delta\leq1\) | Reliance on standalone rather than dependence-adjusted evidence |

The ideal static reference is recovered with correct likelihoods/source baselines, all residual source/feature coefficients zero, \(\kappa=\rho=1\), \(\delta=0\), and \(\beta_N^{\mathrm{ev}}=\beta_C=0\). Model A outside these restrictions is a descriptive accumulation model, not necessarily a coherent Bayesian posterior.

For \(\rho<1\), identical evidence sets can produce presentation-order effects. Apply this interpretation to documents about the same fixed target with dates preserved. Real changes in the world need a transition model; fitting a forgetting coefficient is not a substitute.

Confirmation and negativity are distinct: adverse evidence confirms a bearish assessment and contradicts a bullish assessment. Include both starting positions.

## 3. Model B: joint inference about a thesis and its auxiliaries

For the main Gershman-inspired alternative, represent the relevant joint state explicitly:

- \(H\): favorable underlying company thesis.
- \(A\): a temporary disruption that could explain disappointing observations.
- \(\mathbf V\): informative/uninformative source types for the sources in the episode.

For a simple static episode:

$$
q_t(h,a,\mathbf v)=
\frac{
P(E_t\mid h,a,\mathbf v,D_{t-1})q_{t-1}(h,a,\mathbf v)
}{
\sum_{h',a',\mathbf v'}
P(E_t\mid h',a',\mathbf v',D_{t-1})
q_{t-1}(h',a',\mathbf v')
}.
$$

For example, a continuous operating observation can have:

$$
E_t\mid H=h,A=a,V_{s_t}=1
\sim \mathcal N(\mu_h-da,\sigma_t^2),\qquad d>0,
$$

$$
E_t\mid V_{s_t}=0
\sim \mathcal N(\mu_{\mathrm{junk}},\sigma_{\mathrm{junk}}^2),
$$

with the noise process independent of \(H,A\). Thus a low observation might reflect weak fundamentals, a temporary disruption, or an uninformative source. A later operational check can separately discriminate \(A=1\) from \(A=0\), using a declared check accuracy.

The forecasts and auxiliary assessments are marginals of the same joint distribution:

$$
m_t=P_t(H=1)=\sum_{a,\mathbf v}q_t(1,a,\mathbf v),
$$

$$
P_t(A=1)=\sum_{h,\mathbf v}q_t(h,1,\mathbf v),
\qquad
P_t(V_s=1)=\sum_{h,a,\mathbf v:v_s=1}q_t(h,a,\mathbf v).
$$

The starting joint distribution must be specified. A factorized prior is a deliberate simplifying assumption, not a general claim that the thesis and its auxiliaries are independent. Source-framing coefficients can shift initial source-validity priors once; do not reapply that same prior information at every arrival.

A dynamic extension first predicts through an explicit transition kernel, then performs the observation update. For a fixed terminal target \(H\), transition the temporary conditions and source states as appropriate, rather than silently allowing the definition of \(H\) to change.

This model can reduce source trust while maintaining the central thesis, or vice versa, without an arbitrary bias coefficient. The approach is motivated by [Gershman (2019)](https://gershmanlab.com/pubs/HowToNeverBeWrong.pdf). Task-specific priors on auxiliaries are candidates for fitting, but keeping a thesis is not by itself evidence of rationalization.

Do not compute a posterior source-validity marginal from an item, then plug it into a second independent update of the company hypothesis using that same item. That would reuse evidence. Both marginals should come from the single joint update.

## 4. Output model: elicited probabilities

The agent's report need not be an identity readout of the fitted state. Let:

$$
U_t=b_p+\gamma_p z_t+\beta_N^{\mathrm{out}}W_t+\epsilon_t,
\qquad
\epsilon_t\sim\mathcal N(0,\sigma_p^2),
$$

$$
\hat p_t=\mathcal R_\Delta\left[\sigma(U_t)\right].
$$

Here \(\mathcal R_\Delta\) rounds to a declared probability grid. The parameters are:

- \(b_p\): report intercept; negative values shift reports toward the unfavorable outcome.
- \(\gamma_p>0\): report extremity relative to the fitted state; values above one expand distance from 0.5 on the log-odds scale.
- \(\beta_N^{\mathrm{out}}\): effect of negative response wording, after mapping reports to the same event.
- \(\sigma_p\): residual report variability, encompassing unmodeled effects.

Calling these output parameters is a modeling convention. A response prompt may also change deliberation. We cannot establish a pure verbal-readout mechanism from a wording effect alone.

The rounding model accommodates 0 and 1 as valid observations. For a report bin \([l_t,u_t]\), the likelihood is:

$$
P(\hat p_t\mid z_t)=
\Phi\left(\frac{\operatorname{logit}(u_t)-\mu_t^{p}}{\sigma_p}\right)
-
\Phi\left(\frac{\operatorname{logit}(l_t)-\mu_t^{p}}{\sigma_p}\right),
$$

where \(\mu_t^{p}=b_p+\gamma_p z_t+\beta_N^{\mathrm{out}}W_t\), and logit endpoints are interpreted as infinities. The zero-noise case is the deterministic rounded limit. If a different response-format or endpoint model is used, it must be prespecified.

For source probes, align the target and timing: a prior item-validity question can measure \(r_t\), whereas a post-evidence source question should target the appropriate posterior marginal. Adding freely scaled output mappings for every probe would introduce further identification problems.

## 5. Output model: decisions and downside sensitivity

Start with a simulated binary investment: investing yields \(+G\) if \(H=1\), and \(-L\) if \(H=0\), with \(G,L>0\); not investing yields zero. Define:

$$
\Delta U_t=m_tG-\lambda_L(1-m_t)L,
$$

$$
P(a_t=\mathrm{invest})=
\sigma\left[b_a+\tau_a\Delta U_t+\psi I(a_{t-1}=\mathrm{invest})\right].
$$

\(\lambda_L>0\) is relative loss weighting, \(\tau_a>0\) is choice sensitivity, \(b_a\) is an action intercept and \(\psi\) describes action persistence beyond the modeled payoff. This is a deliberately simplified decision rule, not a full portfolio or prospect-theory model.

With zero action bias/persistence and deterministic choice, the investment threshold is:

$$
m_t>\frac{\lambda_L L}{G+\lambda_L L}.
$$

This isolates a way to become more cautious without changing a probability forecast. Vary \(G\) and \(L\) independently and specify switching costs and portfolio constraints; otherwise action persistence or reluctance may be justified by omitted economics.

For the pilot, also fit the policy conditionally on observed reports, substituting \(\hat p_t\) for \(m_t\). Label this decision/report alignment. It does not establish an underlying utility function or solve the latent-state identification problem.

## 6. Alternative continuous model for forecast uncertainty

Binary event probability alone does not identify a separate confidence or precision parameter. A continuous task can make uncertainty observable.

Let a fundamental quantity \(\theta\) have a Gaussian prior:

$$
\theta\sim\mathcal N(\mu_0,V_0),
\qquad
y_t\mid\theta\sim\mathcal N(\theta,R_t).
$$

For independent observations, allow subjective precision weighting:

$$
\tilde R_t=
R_t\exp\left[-\boldsymbol\beta_X^\top x_t-\beta_N^{\mathrm{ev}}N_t\right],
$$

$$
K_t=\frac{V_{t-1}}{V_{t-1}+\tilde R_t},
\quad
\mu_t=\mu_{t-1}+K_t(y_t-\mu_{t-1}),
\quad
V_t=(1-K_t)V_{t-1}.
$$

Suppose the eventually observed outcome is \(Y=\theta+\xi\), with \(\xi\sim\mathcal N(0,\tau_Y^2)\). The predictive variance is:

$$
S_t^2=V_t+\tau_Y^2.
$$

An output layer for median and prediction interval is:

$$
\widehat Q_{0.5,t}=b_Y+\gamma_Y\mu_t,
$$

$$
[\widehat Q_{0.1,t},\widehat Q_{0.9,t}]
=\widehat Q_{0.5,t}\pm1.28155\,c_I S_t,\qquad c_I>0.
$$

With a correctly specified forecast state and identity median mapping, \(c_I<1\) is interval compression relative to that state. It is not automatically a psychological diagnosis of overconfidence: internal precision and report width can trade off.

If a binary target is \(H=I(Y>c)\), derive its probability from the same continuous distribution:

$$
m_t=1-\Phi\left(\frac{c-\mu_t}{S_t}\right).
$$

Do not fit unrelated binary and continuous latent models and assume their answers form one coherent forecast. Multivariate fundamentals can use a linear-Gaussian extension with an explicit covariance matrix.

Correlated observations require a joint observation covariance or a provenance model. Feeding duplicated measurements into the independent-observation update would itself implement duplicate counting.

When the precision multiplier depends on the realized observation's valence, this is a descriptive adaptive Gaussian update; it is not automatically the posterior of a normalized Gaussian sampling model. The ordinary conjugate reference holds with the declared, correctly specified observation variances. A fully generative alternative must specify and normalize its observation likelihood.

The decomposition of \(S_t^2\) into \(V_t\) and \(\tau_Y^2\) is identifiable only with suitable assumptions and manipulations, such as controlled observation noise and repeated outcomes. A prediction interval alone does not separate epistemic and outcome uncertainty.

## 7. Optional active-research model

For a research item \(j\) with cost \(c_j\), define expected value of information under the agent's modeled distribution:

$$
\operatorname{VOI}_t(j)=
\mathbb E_{e\sim P_j(\cdot\mid D_t)}
\left[\max_a\mathbb E[U(a,H)\mid D_t,e]\right]
-
\max_a\mathbb E[U(a,H)\mid D_t]
-c_j.
$$

A stochastic acquisition policy is:

$$
P(J_t=j)\propto
\exp\left[\tau_I\operatorname{VOI}_t(j)+b_j\right],
$$

with a stop option having score zero as the reference. This separates decision-relevant information value from preferences for particular research formats. Use a common modeled information set; a benchmark that knows hidden source truth has an advantage and must be labeled accordingly.

## 8. Worked example: distinct routes to an adverse response

Take \(p_0=0.70\), item validity \(r=0.80\), informative accuracy 0.90 and uninformative accuracy 0.50. A negative report gives:

$$
\ell=\log(0.18/0.82)=-1.51635,
\qquad
p_{\mathrm{reference}}
=\sigma(\operatorname{logit}(0.70)+\ell)
=0.33871.
$$

| Change from reference | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Greater perceived item validity, \(r=0.90\) | Assessment probability 0.27528 |
| Double weight on negative evidence, \(\beta_N^{\mathrm{ev}}=\log2\) | Assessment probability 0.10107 |
| Output log-odds shift of -0.75 only | Same modeled assessment 0.33871; reported probability 0.19481 |
| Relative loss weight rises from 1 to 2, with \(G=2,L=1\) | Same probability; investment threshold rises from 1/3 to 1/2 |

In the last row, the reference probability is just above the first threshold and below the second, so the preferred deterministic action changes without any forecast change.

Under the unmodified joint item-validity model, the same observation lowers posterior validity from 0.80 to about 0.73118. This illustrates why a content-driven change in source trust can follow ordinary joint inference.

The example values were numerically checked. It illustrates model distinctions, not proof that data from one episode can identify which mechanism produced a response.

## 9. Identification: restrictions are essential

Even the simplest model has an exact scale ambiguity. With neutral starting odds, no forgetting and fixed likelihood ratios:

$$
z_t=\kappa\sum_{j\leq t}\ell_j,
\qquad
\operatorname{logit}(\hat p_t)=b_p+\gamma_p\kappa\sum_{j\leq t}\ell_j.
$$

Only \(\gamma_p\kappa\) is identified. Doubling evidence sensitivity and halving report gain produces the same probability reports. More examples of the same type do not resolve this symmetry.

Source credibility, evidence weight and numeric interval width can likewise compensate for one another. Source/probability/action probes help only with explicit assumptions about their mappings. They are not direct measurements of latent belief states.

Necessary design contrasts include:

| Quantity | Contrast or restriction |
| --- | --- |
| Source-framing effect | Randomized skeptical/positive source profiles with equal track records and matched factual content; appropriately timed trust probes |
| Negative-evidence weight | Equally diagnostic favorable and unfavorable facts, with source/framing crossed |
| Congruence | Both bullish and bearish starting assessments |
| Duplicate sensitivity | Independent corroboration versus mechanically repeated evidence, with provenance controlled |
| Retention/order | Reorder a fixed dated evidence set; vary sequence length |
| Reporting effects | Vary response wording/format with the available evidence fixed; separate calibration tasks; fix one scale in the primary fit |
| Loss weighting | Cross gains and losses while holding the information set fixed |
| Auxiliary explanation | Evidence distinguishing the auxiliary from the main thesis, plus selective marginal/conditional probes |
| Uncertainty | Known-noise controls and repeated continuous outcomes; constrain inference or reporting precision rather than freeing both without anchors |

For the first model comparisons, fix \(\gamma_p=1\) and avoid a simultaneous free prior-scaling parameter. An estimated \(\kappa\) is then evidence sensitivity on the chosen reporting scale, not a proven internal parameter. Fit an alternative constrained output model separately if needed. If candidate mechanisms remain observationally equivalent, report a combined behavioral sensitivity.

## 10. Fitting plan and smallest useful family

Use the actual observation likelihood: rounded logistic-normal probability reports, Bernoulli choices, and an appropriate observation model for quantiles/trust probes. Conditional independence of output channels is an assumption to check; reports from the same step may share residual variation.

For parameters \(\Theta\), a working likelihood is:

$$
\mathcal L(\Theta)=
\prod_i\prod_t
p(\hat p_{it},a_{it},\hat r_{it},\widehat Q_{it}\mid
D_{it},\Theta),
$$

with dependence carried through each episode's state/history. A conditional product over time does not make the observations independent replications. Add parameter sharing/hierarchical structure only where repeated episodes/configurations support it.

Compare a small family:

1. Reference inference with constrained reporting.
2. Weighted updating: free overall evidence sensitivity, negative-evidence weight and duplicate sensitivity.
3. Source impressions: add one randomized framing effect, with aligned source probes.
4. Output-framing alternative: a restricted model allowing response-framing shifts.
5. Joint thesis/auxiliary inference: Model B under explicit prior and likelihood alternatives.

Fit decision/report alignment separately at first. Keep forgetting, congruence, multiple format coefficients and source-learning rates as staged extensions, not a large freely fitted initial parameter vector. Use the continuous model when interval forecasts become a primary endpoint.

Before agent comparisons, simulate from each candidate over plausible parameters, test parameter recovery and model confusion, inspect trade-offs, and choose episode counts using simulation. This follows [Wilson and Collins (2019)](https://elifesciences.org/articles/49547); recovery validates a best-case fitting procedure, not the psychological account itself.

Hold out complete episodes and scenario families, compare predictive likelihood of observed responses, and bootstrap or otherwise quantify uncertainty at episode/run level. Separately assess outcome forecasts with proper scoring rules and decisions under the declared mandate. [Gneiting and Raftery (2007)](https://sites.stat.washington.edu/people/raftery/Research/PDF/Gneiting2007jasa.pdf) provide the scoring-rule foundation.

Do not fix the sign of asymmetry in advance. Findings in specific LLM instrumental-learning tasks show that updating asymmetries can depend on task framing and agency; they do not establish a universal negativity parameter. See [Schubert et al. (2024)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.03969).

The next deliverable should be a concrete episode with the latent-state table, likelihoods, provenance graph and randomized variants specified well enough to simulate these candidate models. Only then should experimental implementation and the versioned report contract change.
