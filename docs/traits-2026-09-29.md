## Question

The aim is general traits: parameters that describe a configuration across tasks, so that measuring them on some tasks predicts behaviour on others. The earlier second-layer analysis concluded that noticing does not carry over between hidden structures. That analysis asked how much one configuration's threshold varied across structures, which mixes two different things:
- **Task effects:** some structures are easier to notice for every configuration, for example selection records that make the structure plain. This says nothing against a trait.
- **Configuration × task interaction:** configurations changing places from one structure to another. This is what would count against a trait.

This reanalysis separates the two for every candidate trait for which the sessions give a per-session value.

## Method

**Decomposition** (a generalizability study; crossed random effects, Gibbs sampling, half-normal priors on the standard deviations):

y = μ + configuration + task + configuration × task + session noise

- **A task** is a hidden structure in one format, nine in all:
  - relay and disclosure, in paired cases, formal cases and dossiers;
  - copying, selection and mismatch, in urns.
- **Consistency** ρ = σ²config / (σ²config + σ²config×task). It is the share of a configuration's deviation from the average that it carries to every task.

**Transfer** is scored directly, leave one task out. A configuration's deviation from the other configurations on a held-out task is predicted from its mean deviation on the other tasks.
- **Transfer gain** = 1 − MSE(prediction) / MSE(predicting no deviation).
- **Sign agreement** is the share of held-out cells where the predicted direction is right.

**Candidate traits and where each is read:**

| Trait | Per-session value | Sessions that measure it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Precision | log report noise τ | every forecasting session |
| Evidence weight | γ | every forecasting session |
| Default for a named structure whose rate is not given | log-odds prior at the uninformative description | sessions where the structure is named and its rate asked for or implied: paired cases, formal cases, dossiers, asked and probed dossiers, asked and probed corrected urns |
| Cue sensitivity | log-odds range, strongly reassuring → strongly suggestive | the same |
| Stated–applied gap | mean gap between stated and applied rates at the ambiguous levels | sessions that asked for rates |
| Noticing threshold | per-structure θ from the structure checks (with its uncertainty) | per-structure second-layer fits, Astra and Sol only |

**Exclusions:**
- **Case order:** formal cue sessions with a designed case order are left out.
- **Urn priors:** read only from the corrected texts (copying and selection urn2, mismatch urn3). Every urn version counts for precision.

**The data are unbalanced.**
- **Astra and Sol:** about 54 sessions each, across all nine tasks.
- **Luna and Terra:** 7 each, in the paired, formal and dossier tasks only.
- **Astra-low and Sol-low:** the paired tasks only.
- **Confound:** configuration and task are therefore partly confounded. The urn tasks exist only for the frontier pair.

## Interpretation

1. **Precision is a general trait.**
   - **Consistency and transfer:** configurations keep their order across tasks (ρ 0.91), and a configuration's precision on other tasks predicts it on a held-out task (gain 0.59, direction right in 85% of cells).
   - **Among the frontier pair it is weaker but present:** Astra is more precise than Sol in most tasks (gain 0.29, 78%).
   - **Parallel in computational psychiatry:** it is the analogue of decision noise in those models.
   - **A floor effect:** the report-noise grid stops at 0.05, where many frontier sessions sit, so differences among the most precise configurations are compressed.
2. **Stated–applied fidelity is a candidate between model families, not within the frontier pair.** Luna's forecasts depart from the rates it states more than the other configurations' do, in all four tasks where it was asked (by 0.05–0.16). Astra, Sol and Terra stay close to their stated rates. Within the frontier pair there is nothing to transfer, because both are close to 0 everywhere.
3. **Noticing is more trait-like than the hierarchical analysis suggested.**
   - **Direction:** Astra needs less prompting than Sol in three of the four structures with valid fits.
   - **The fourth is at ceiling:** both configurations notice selective reporting almost at once, so it cannot separate them.
   - **What the earlier result reflected:** the large spread in the hierarchical fit was mostly task effects, where every configuration finds some structures easier. The misread mismatch wording added to it.
   - **Status:** with two configurations and wide intervals, this is a lead, not a result.
4. **Some candidates cannot show a trait yet, because nothing varies.**
   - **Default for an unstated rate:** every configuration sets about 50%, with Sol slightly lower and noisier. It looks like a shared default, not a difference between configurations.
   - **Evidence weight:** γ ≈ 1 everywhere.
5. **Cue sensitivity is not consistent across tasks.** Sol is more sensitive than Astra, and the GPT-5.6 configurations less, but the order changes between tasks, so it does not transfer here.

## What this means for the abstract battery

1. **Cross every configuration with every task,** with at least two sessions per cell. The current data confound configuration with task: urns exist only for the frontier pair, and the weaker configurations only have the older tasks.
2. **Include configurations that differ.** The frontier pair is at ceiling or identical on several candidates. Traits can only be shown where configurations vary: the low-effort settings and the GPT-5.6 pair should take every task.
3. **Target the candidates that look general:**
   - precision;
   - stated–applied fidelity;
   - noticing threshold;
   - possibly cue sensitivity.

   The unstated-rate default and evidence weight should stay as measured constants unless new configurations vary on them.
4. **Keep every structure away from ceiling and floor for every configuration.** Selective reporting cannot separate configurations if all notice it at once. Matching the cue's evidential strength across structures is one way to do this. Another is a graded cue whose strength varies within each structure.
5. **Extend the report-noise grid below 0.05,** so precise configurations are not all at the floor.
6. **Preregister the leave-one-task-out transfer test,** with the transfer gain and sign agreement as the outcomes, and the configurations and tasks crossed in advance.

## Caveats

- Exploratory and after the fact. Every value here was already known in some form.
- With two to six configurations, variance between configurations is poorly identified. The consistency intervals are wide.
- Tasks are partly confounded with configurations (see the method section above).
- Several traits are read from summary means of grid posteriors, without their uncertainty. The noticing thresholds carry their uncertainty.
